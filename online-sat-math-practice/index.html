Why online practice works for the digital SAT

The SAT is now taken on a laptop or tablet in the Bluebook app, so practising on a screen is closer to test day than practising on paper. You read the question on screen, you use the Desmos calculator built into the app, and you type answers for the student-produced response questions. Two habits carry over directly from online practice: reading the question twice before choosing an answer, and checking with the graphing calculator when a question is about a graph, intercepts or a system of equations.

Instant feedback matters more than the medium. If you only find out your score at the end of a 44-question test, you have already forgotten what you were thinking on question 7. Checking each question as you go lets you fix the reasoning while it's fresh, which is the point of practice in the first place.

Worked example 1: a linear model in words

A gym charges a $25 sign-up fee plus $15 a month. After how many months will a member have paid $160 in total?

Write the total as a function of months m: total = 25 + 15m. Set it equal to 160: 25 + 15m = 160, so 15m = 135 and m = 9. Check: 25 + 15 × 9 = 25 + 135 = 160. ✓

On the SAT the same model is often asked backwards: "what does 15 represent?" (the monthly cost) or "what does 25 represent?" (the one-off fee). The constant term is the starting value and the coefficient is the rate.

Worked example 2: a quadratic you can factor or graph

What are the solutions of x² + 2x − 15 = 0?

Find two numbers that multiply to −15 and add to 2: they are 5 and −3. So x² + 2x − 15 = (x + 5)(x − 3), and x = −5 or x = 3.

The graph shows a shortcut the SAT likes: the vertex is exactly halfway between the roots, at x = (−5 + 3)/2 = −1, and its y-value is (−1)² + 2(−1) − 15 = −16. If a question asks for the minimum value of the function, that's −16.

Worked example 3: two percent changes in a row

A price rises by 10% and then by another 20%. What is the total percent increase?

It is not 30%. Each change multiplies the price: a 10% rise multiplies by 1.10 and a 20% rise by 1.20. Together that is 1.10 × 1.20 = 1.32, a 32% increase. In the same way, a 20% rise followed by a 20% fall leaves you at 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96, which is 4% below where you started.

Answer key

  1. 7: Subtract 3: 5x = 35, so x = 7.
  2. 2: Solve for y: y = 2x − 5, so the slope is 2.
  3. 24: 3(9) − 3 = 27 − 3 = 24.
  4. 5: x² − 9x + 20 = (x − 4)(x − 5), so the solutions are 4 and 5.
  5. 25%: The discount is $15, and 15/60 = 0.25 = 25%. Divide by the original price.
  6. 8: Add the equations: 3x = 12, so x = 4 and y = 2. Then xy = 8.
  7. 12: 13² − 5² = 169 − 25 = 144, and √144 = 12.
  8. 25π: 2πr = 10π gives r = 5, so the area is π(5²) = 25π.
  9. 48: 2⁴ = 16 and 3 × 16 = 48. The 3 is not raised to the power.
  10. 12: The four numbers must add to 4 × 9 = 36, and 36 − 24 = 12.
